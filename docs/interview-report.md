## Executive Summary

This interview explored the experience of a 59-year-old individual with lifelong face recognition difficulties (likely developmental prosopagnosia). The conversation revealed a moderate, context-dependent form of the condition that significantly impacts social situations. A clear product direction emerged: a **private training app** to improve analytical face perception skills, rather than real-time facial recognition technology.

## Background

### First Awareness

The participant's earliest memory of recognizing the condition occurred around age 13. After a parent-teacher meeting, they could not identify which of two known teachers they had just been speaking with once the teacher walked away. This moment crystallized a pattern they had likely been unconsciously managing for years.

> "I had to admit to my dad that I could not figure out which of those two teachers we were just speaking to just then, after he left."

### Recognition Pattern

The participant experiences **context-dependent recognition**:

- **Recognizes reliably:** Parents, sister, wife, closest friends
- **Recognizes in context:** Coworkers at work, people in expected places/times
- **Struggles with:** Same people in unexpected contexts (after-work socials, different clothes)
- **Cannot recognize:** People known years ago but not seen recently, actors in films

### Additional Factors

- Poor memory for names
- Difficulty remembering details of conversations
- Suspects autism spectrum and ADHD (undiagnosed)
- Married someone with "a very recognizable face" (possibly unconscious selection)
- Dated late in life, acknowledges face blindness would have made dating difficult

## Key Findings

### 1. Context is Everything

Recognition works when context supports it. The same coworker who is instantly recognizable at their desk becomes uncertain at a bar after work. Remove context, and recognition collapses.

> "Suddenly they are in different clothes, acting differently, and if I'm meeting them in a bar, I would likely see somebody and think, I think that's so-and-so, but I can't be sure."

### 2. The "Second Meetup" Problem

First meetings at new groups go well - everyone is new, expectations are clear. Second meetings are highly stressful because:

- Some people are new, some have been met before
- No way to distinguish who is who
- Risk of offending someone by not remembering them
- Risk of over-familiarity with a stranger

This leads to **avoidance behavior** - skipping events entirely.

### 3. Universal Disbelief

When the participant discloses their condition, the response is consistently disbelief. There is no established social understanding or accommodation script for face blindness, unlike other conditions.

> "It's not an accepted condition even if I tell somebody."

### 4. The Cost

- Closed opportunities (past and future)
- Exhausting cognitive load in social situations
- Social situations are already challenging (possible autism spectrum)
- Face blindness is "probably the biggest problem"

### 5. Current Coping Strategies

| Strategy | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Context reliance | Expect to see certain people at certain places/times |
| Passive waiting | Let others recognize them first, watch for recognition cues |
| Social co-pilot | Wife attends events and identifies people |
| Multi-modal recognition | Voice, stance, movement, clothes, mannerisms |
| Avoidance | Skip stressful social situations |

### 6. The Movie Problem

Difficulty tracking actors in films - frequently asks "Is that the same person from the last scene?" This demonstrates the issue is truly face processing, not memory or context, since the same actor appears minutes apart.

## Product Direction

### What the User Wants

**A private training app to improve underlying face perception ability.**

Not:
- Real-time facial recognition ("creepy")
- Database of real people to identify
- Something used visibly in social situations

But rather:
- Teaching how to analytically observe faces
- Learning to notice distinguishing features
- Progressive difficulty (simple → realistic)
- Building skill like an artist trains their eye

### The "Artist's Eye" Hypothesis

The participant proposed that artists are trained to really *look* at things and understand structure. Could the same approach work for faces?

> "An artist has to really be trained to look, maybe I can be trained to look at faces and notice the differences?"

This aligns with **feature-based analytical processing** - a compensatory strategy where individuals consciously analyze individual features rather than relying on automatic holistic face processing.

### Key Feature Ideas

1. **Progressive training exercises**
   - Start with stylized/simplified faces
   - Move to realistic photographs
   - Eventually include faces of known people (opt-in)

2. **Analytical observation training**
   - Focus on individual features (brow, nose, ears, jaw)
   - Compare and contrast exercises
   - "Spot the difference" with faces

3. **Pre-event preparation** (secondary feature)
   - Study photos and names before social events
   - Review who might be present

4. **Personal notes database** (if privacy concerns addressed)
   - Discreet way to record distinguishing features
   - Review before seeing someone again

### User Commitment

The participant confirmed willingness to practice regularly (e.g., 10 minutes daily) if there's evidence it could help. They requested research into:

- Scientific opinion on training effectiveness
- Others' experiences
- What interventions have shown promise
